# Post-tensioned sections under axial force and biaxial bending

Feasibility study, 2026-11-04. This packet covers Korean KDS for buildings and bridges and records two independent calculations. The first bridge implementation is planned against AASHTO LRFD with a clause comparison for projects using KDS. The current product boundary excludes prestressing and the existing RC profiles remain development previews. [Bridge delivery plan](2026-11-04-aashto-lrfd-post-tensioned-sections-plan.md) · [Product scope, §§3–4](../engineering/01-product-scope-and-workflow.md)

## First section the application should be able to check

Start with a **bonded, internally post-tensioned section at ULS**, after grouting and effective losses. At a specified cut, accept a connected polygonal concrete section with openings, ordinary reinforcement if present, and one or more eccentric tendons with known effective states. An I-section, a box with an asymmetric opening and a building beam use the same section coordinates. Tendon forces at different positions contribute independently to both moments. Published biaxial section analyses provide a comparison method for this generalization. [Rodriguez-Gutierrez and Aristizabal-Ochoa, ACI Structural Journal, 2000](https://www.concrete.org/publications/internationalconcreteabstractsportal.aspx?id=7420&m=details)

Qualify the first implementation for sections where plane sections apply, away from anchors, deviators and other disturbed regions. Require effective bond, known duct occupancy and strands locally parallel to the section normal. Record the cut and tangent even for a draped tendon. An inclined cut through a tendon requires both force projection and a compatible strain model. Anchorage-zone resistance, shear, torsion, slab punching, member stability and construction-stage analysis require separate checks.

| Condition at the cut | What a section check can establish |
|---|---|
| Internal, grouted and bonded PT | Subsequent local strain increments determine the change in tendon strain. With the effective reference state, material laws and qualified KDS route, a biaxial resistance domain is feasible. |
| Internal, permanently unbonded PT | Tendon stress gain generally depends on deformation along the tendon. A later check could use a documented member-analysis stress for the same combination and limit state, or a separately qualified, restricted KDS approximation. A stress supplied for one combination supports only that conditional check. |
| Before grouting, external tendon, partial bond or uncertain grouting | Keep each status distinct. A bonded-system designation on a drawing does not establish bond at every stage. External tendons need the geometry and deformation between contact points. Unknown bond, development or stage blocks the first route, including a mixed section with any unsupported tendon. |
| Pretensioning | The bonded core can be reused where stress is fully transferred and reinforcement is developed. Release, transfer/development length and debonded lengths need a distinct input and applicability check. |

The bridge standard explicitly distinguishes the pre-grout state, permanent unbonded tendons and external contact intervals. The Florida DOT experimental research also documents the member dependence of unbonded tendon stress. [KDS 24 14 21:2025, §§1.5.7.5–6 and 4.1.1.2(6)](https://www.codil.or.kr/filebank/moct2014/202508/MOCT3437_2.PDF?nserialno=3437) · [Consolazio, Hamilton and Pérez-Avilés, 2022](https://rosap.ntl.bts.gov/view/dot/75096)

## Mechanics and engineering input

**One effective state per tendon at the cut.** Record tendon and parent-region IDs, section/station, coordinates in mm, actual steel area `Ap` in mm², material/strand certificate and grade, duct boundary, grout and bond state, local direction, stage/date, and evidence of development. Record effective tensile stress `fpe` in MPa or effective force in N with the loss calculation or measurement that supplies it. If both are supplied they must reconcile through `Ap`. Jack force, anchorage force and effective force at the cut are distinct. Friction/wobble, seating, sequential stressing and elastic shortening, creep, shrinkage and relaxation belong to the upstream loss record. Local strength evaluation consumes the selected state once. [KDS 14 20 60:2022, §§4.2–4.3](https://www.kcsc.re.kr/standardCode/viewer/KDS%2014%2020%2060:2022-01-11) · [KDS 24 14 21:2025, §§1.5.7.2–4](https://www.codil.or.kr/filebank/moct2014/202508/MOCT3437_2.PDF?nserialno=3437)

**Bonded strain convention.** The following is a proposed kinematic contract for a fixed stage, perfect bond, longitudinal strands and no further free-strain evolution. Use signed strain and stress, compression positive. At reference stage `r`, the physical tendon law and its history must reproduce `σp,r = −fpe`. Store the compatible section strain at the tendon at the same stage and measure the strain plane from the declared origin.

```text
esec(q,p) = e0 + kx (yp − y0) + ky (xp − x0)
ep(q,p)   = ep,r + [esec(q,p) − esec(r,p)]
Fp        = Ap σp(ep)                          [N]
Mxp       = Fp (yp − y0),  Myp = Fp (xp − x0) [N·mm]
```

The bracket is the common strain **increment** after bond. Returning to stage `r` must recover every effective tendon force and the external reference actions of the whole section. Those actions may include sustained loads. Obtain the concrete plane and locked-in material state from a documented stage analysis or solve reference equilibrium with declared physical laws, actions and history. A section formulation referenced to decompressed concrete can store the equivalent offset `ep,r − esec(r,p)` after this mapping is established. Using `−fpe/Ep` for that offset requires justification for both the steel law and the concrete reference strain. Keep physical reference-state calibration separate from ULS material factoring. [Project signs and origin](../engineering/02-data-conventions-and-terminology.md) · [FHWA PT manual, §7.3.2.1](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/bridge/concrete/hif15016.pdf) · [PCI, flexural strength and strain at decompression, Eq. 2](https://www.pci.org/PCI_Docs/Design_Resources/Guides_and_manuals/references/bridge_design_manual/JL-88-September-October_Flexural_Strength_of_Prestressed_Concrete_Members.pdf)

**Steel, grout and concrete.** A prestressing-steel law needs the actual tensile stress–strain relation, proof/yield definition, ultimate strength, admissible strain, unloading/compression policy and source. Keep total mechanical tendon strain, local concrete strain, and any code-defined net tensile strain for classification as separate quantities. An ordinary rebar `fy` or a curve mirrored into compression changes the resistance. Evaluate strands separately where eccentricity, strain gradient or effective stress differs. A centroidal bundle approximation needs an error check under biaxial curvature.

Assign each area exactly once. A physical box opening is a void. A tendon duct has an envelope, steel area and stage-dependent fill. For the first grouted pilot, declare whether grout contributes and under which law. Net host concrete plus explicitly participating grout and full steel is one reproducible representation. A gross-area implementation needs an independently reconciled displaced-material term at the **host** strain. Deducting a duct as a hole and subtracting its displaced material again loses area twice. The exact rings and occupied regions control properties, containment and extreme fibres. [Geometry owner](../engineering/03-geometry-and-reinforcement.md) · [KDS 14 20 60:2022, §4.1.1(6) on pre-bond duct area](https://www.kcsc.re.kr/standardCode/viewer/KDS%2014%2020%2060:2022-01-11)

**Demand.** For a total internal-force section ledger, the primary prestress is already present in the concrete and tendon forces. Identify the free body of the source analysis and whether each imported combination contains equivalent tendon loads, primary prestress, or secondary restraint reactions. Transform once to the common centroidal basis. Preserve applicable secondary effects and their combination factors. In a simply supported free section with prestress alone the external vector is zero. In a continuous girder the restraint moment can be nonzero at the same cut. A total FE prestress moment and a bare `P·e` from the drawing cannot be added indiscriminately. The bridge profile must reconcile its action-side prestress rules with this internal-force representation through an equilibrium example before comparing demand and resistance. [KDS 14 20 60:2022, §4.5.1(3)](https://www.kcsc.re.kr/standardCode/viewer/KDS%2014%2020%2060:2022-01-11) · [KDS 24 14 21:2025, §§1.5.7.1, 1.5.7.5–6](https://www.codil.or.kr/filebank/moct2014/202508/MOCT3437_2.PDF?nserialno=3437) · [FHWA, Fig. 3.16 and §7.3.1](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/bridge/concrete/hif15016.pdf)

## Independent checks and delivery order

**1. Prove the reference state and signs.** A manufactured symmetric net concrete/grout area of 50,000 mm² carries 6 MPa uniform compression with an elastic modulus of 30,000 MPa. Two longitudinal tendons of 150 mm² each lie at `(40, −80)` and `(−40, 80)` mm about the centroid. Give each a synthetic elastic modulus of 200,000 MPa and an effective tensile stress magnitude of 1,000 MPa. These are test inputs. The reference concrete strain is +0.0002, tendon strain is −0.005 and the offset is −0.0052.

| State | Independent expectation |
|---|---|
| Reference, no external load | Host +300 kN, each tendon −150 kN. The complete section has `(P, Mx, My) = (0, 0, 0)`. Omitting the reference concrete strain produces a spurious +12 kN. |
| Apply curvature increments `Δkx = 5 × 10⁻⁶/mm`, `Δky = 2.5 × 10⁻⁶/mm`, `Δe0 = 0` | Tendon forces become −159 and −141 kN. The **tendon pair alone** gives `(−300 kN, +1.44 kN·m, −0.72 kN·m)`. |

Both rows were checked with rational arithmetic independently of the repository kernels. Next test a single eccentric tendon with balancing concrete curvature, true holes and multiple off-axis tendon groups. Verify all three resultants against independent area integrals, finite-difference tangents away from breakpoints, translated origins, reflected layouts, and equivalence of net and reconciled gross ledgers.

**2. Verify the uniaxial strength calculation before building a surface.** FHWA's worked post-tensioned box girder at Node 25 provides an external benchmark with a stated tendon characteristic and compression block. Using its printed inputs and stress law, independent scalar bisection of axial equilibrium gives `c = 6.83512 in`, `fps = 268.299 ksi`, `Mn = 82,986.7 kip·ft` for the example without mild reinforcement. The publication's trial `c = 6.86 in` leaves about 61 kip between the printed forces, and the two subsequent moment figures differ. Compare on the same geometry, strain reference, constitutive laws and units. This checks section arithmetic only. A Korean code verdict needs a separate KDS calculation for each route. [FHWA-HIF-15-016, §7.3.5, printed pp. 159–161](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/bridge/concrete/hif15016.pdf)

**3. Qualify separate bridge and building pilots.** Use the bridge box from the AASHTO plan, with ducts in both webs, unequal effective forces, an opening and oblique bending. For the building fixture, use a beam with passive steel and an eccentric grouted tendon. Obtain adopted KDS design sheets for both. Independently integrate reference section states and reproduce profile-specific resistance, including pure compression/tension, pure bending, combined actions, the controlling material and boundary transitions. Compare full biaxial states with an independent section algorithm. Use measured ultimate loads and observed failure modes for physical validation, recording the actual losses and loading history. Require independent Korean-code structural review, surface convergence and fail-closed checks before issuing Design verdicts. [Published biaxial method and five examples](https://www.concrete.org/publications/internationalconcreteabstractsportal.aspx?id=7420&m=details) · [Project V&V](../09-verification-validation-and-release.md)

**4. Extend by engineering use case.** Next qualify internal unbonded PT for a specific building or bridge use case with the additional member inputs and its own test cases. In a separate service workflow check concrete and steel stresses at stressing and after losses, then decompression and cracking, using physical materials and service combinations. Add pretensioning only after transfer/development evidence is available. The immediate work product is this research packet. Production schema, kernels, profiles and reports have no PT implementation or KDS PT validation from this study.

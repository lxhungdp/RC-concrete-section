# AASHTO LRFD plan for post-tensioned bridge sections

The first delivery should let a bridge designer enter a cut through a girder with **internal, bonded post-tensioning**, supply the effective prestress and factored section actions, and inspect the governing strength check in `P–Mx–My`. Prove a rectangular case first, then a closed box with an asymmetric opening, unequal tendon forces and bending about both axes. Use the same section model for KDS bridge projects. The design basis selects and identifies the provisions that govern each result.

## Design basis for the first profile

Use the **AASHTO LRFD Bridge Design Specifications, 10th edition (2024), with the September 2025 errata** as the planning baseline for highway bridges. The publisher lists that correction and identifies substantial changes in Sections 3 and 5. Record the edition actually adopted by the bridge owner, amendments and source units with the project. For a Korean project using KDS and AASHTO together, record the KDS edition, which articles incorporate AASHTO, precedence for overrides and the engineer's approval. The code name alone cannot resolve those choices. [AASHTO publication and errata](https://store.transportation.org/Item/PublicationDetail?ID=5377) · [AASHTO edition announcement](https://aashtojournal.transportation.org/aashto-issues-10th-lrfd-bridge-design-spec-edition/) · [KDS 24 14 21:2025](https://www.codil.or.kr/filebank/moct2014/202508/MOCT3437_2.PDF?nserialno=3437)

The public AASHTO contents and revision list establish the article map below. Full corrected clauses, equations, commentary, applicability and owner amendments still require review against an authorized copy. Until then the common mechanics can be tested with specified laws. A proposed `PT_DESIGN_BASIS_UNQUALIFIED` issue would identify the missing article or approval and block a Design verdict. Sources consulted 2026-11-04. [AASHTO 10th-edition contents and index](https://store.transportation.org/Common/DownloadContentFiles?id=2493)

| Tendon and effective state | §§5.4.2–4, 5.4.6, 5.9.2–3 | Compare KDS §§1.5.7.2–4, 3.3. Share area, position, duct geometry, effective-state fields and loss provenance. Qualify material grade, constitutive law, stress limits and factors by profile. |
| Strength of a general section | §§5.6.2, 5.6.3.1.1, 5.6.3.2.4–5 | Compare KDS §§4.1.1.1–3. Share bonded strain increments and integration of axial force and both moments. Each profile owns concrete and tendon limits, and the applicability of fibre or block mechanics. Rectangular/flanged beam shortcuts require their specific geometry and action domain. |
| Compression, tension and resistance factors | §§5.5.4.2, 5.6.3.2.1, 5.6.4.4–5, 5.6.4.7, 5.6.6 | Compare KDS §§1.4.2.3, 1.4.3.2, 3.3.3, 4.1.1.2–3. Keep the AASHTO nominal-to-factored strength calculation distinct from the KDS design-material calculation. Check axial endpoints, net strain measure, symmetry assumptions, thin walls and any reduction or limit for each applicable case. |
| Unbonded and mixed reinforcement | §§5.6.3.1.2, 5.6.3.1.3a–b | Compare KDS §§1.5.7.5–6, 4.1.1.2(6). The AASHTO articles distinguish an all-unbonded flexural case from a mixed one. Qualify the member inputs, range and failure modes of each method before enabling it. |

A project using both documents needs a clause-by-clause record. For example, the KDS bridge text contains an unresolved cross-reference for the tendon diagram. A validated AASHTO law can be used by an AASHTO profile. Using it to resolve that Korean provision requires a documented governing interpretation. ## Delivery scope and engineering inputs

This plan was detailed against `4709046` plus the working-tree changes present on 2026-11-04. Two milestones have different users. The first gives the numerical reviewer per-material resultants for prescribed strain planes. The second gives the bridge designer a **preview** of bonded PT resistance and the check for an imported strength combination. Engineering release has its own approval gate.

The pilot covers one connected concrete host with one or more polygonal openings, explicit duct occupancy, passive bars if present, and developed internal strands at a section where plane sections apply. The strand tangents must be parallel to the section normal at the cut. Use one documented post-grouting effective stage. Composite construction, segmental joints, anchor/deviator regions and thin-wall cases outside the approved domain return a scope issue. Effective flange width, member stability and global load effects come from the bridge model. [Scope owner](../engineering/01-product-scope-and-workflow.md) · [Geometry owner](../engineering/03-geometry-and-reinforcement.md)

| Contract to review before coding | Required input and failure behavior |
|---|---|
| Section and materials | Stable region, duct, tendon and material IDs. Store the cut, axes, origin, real steel areas and strand positions, duct boundaries, bond/development evidence, grout stage and participating material laws. Distinguish the duct envelope from the steel area. Check full containment, overlaps, declared grouping and material domains. An unsupported member of a mixed group blocks the section check. |
| Effective state | Start with an imported equilibrated reference snapshot. Require the stage, loss source, effective force or stress at this cut, the compatible local section strain and the physical material states needed to replay it. Grout has its own activation reference when it begins to participate. Both supplied force and stress must agree through the actual steel area. Calibrate signed tendon strain on the physical law and replay all three external resultants, including any sustained actions. Missing loss/stage data, an ambiguous inverse material branch or a failed replay blocks use of the state. |
| Actions | Require a factored strength combination, source-model cut and free body, units/origin and prestress accounting. Accept reconciled external section actions. For FE input containing equivalent tendon loads require a documented primary/secondary decomposition with consistent signs and factors. If it cannot be reconciled, keep the source actions for inspection and withhold utilization. |
| Results | Retain the reference state, strain plane, per-entity forces, signed deductions for displaced concrete or grout, nominal and Design totals, controlling entity/limit, source clauses, query basis and uncertainty. Preserve distinct outcomes for an invalid input, unavailable check, indeterminate boundary case and demonstrated exceedance. A missing crossing supplies no numerical ratio. |

## Two fixtures to freeze before production coding

**Full bonded section, V2–V4.** All inputs in this paragraph are manufactured. Use a 200 × 200 mm square centred at drawing `(0,0)`, with a 40 × 40 mm through-opening centred at `(48,−48)`. The centroid after removing the opening is `O = (−2,2) mm`. Place two 20 × 20 mm square ducts at `(+40,+60)` and `(−40,−60)` **relative to O**. Exclude both ducts from the host. Each contains gross grout and a 100 mm² point tendon that displaces grout at the compatible grout strain. The net host area is 37,600 mm² and the participating grout area is 600 mm². Use synthetic linear moduli of 30,000 MPa for concrete, 20,000 MPa for grout and 200,000 MPa for tendon steel. In this fixture both continuum laws use the stated plane as their elastic strain.

| Quantity | Effective state | Trial state |
|---|---:|---:|
| `(e0, kx, ky)` about O, curvature in mm⁻¹ | `(0.0002, 0, 0)` | `(0.00025, 0.000001, −0.0000005)` |
| Effective tensile magnitude per tendon / fixed offset | 1,140 MPa / −0.0059 | same offset |
| Signed forces in the two tendons (N), in listed order | −114,000 / −114,000 | −112,200 / −113,800 |
| **Whole section** `P, Mx, My` (N, N·mm, N·mm) | `0, 0, 0` | `59000, 11663200/3, −5337200/3` |

For reproduction, integrate each rectangle's linear stress and its two first moments, subtract the hole and the duct host material, add the grout, and add the two signed `Ap(σp − σgrout)` point terms. During planning these values were calculated two ways using rational arithmetic, once from rectangular area moments and once using tensor-product Simpson quadrature over each rectangle. This exercises the specified lumped-strand area convention. Comparing a physically distributed strand with that convention is a separate approximation study.

**Interior tendon governs, V5.** For an isolated material-domain test, two tendons of the same synthetic grade have local `y = −60` and `−20 mm`, total-strain offsets `−0.004` and `−0.011`, and a common signed tensile limit of `−0.012`. Use `e0 = ky = 0` with other limits inactive. At `kx = 1/20000 mm⁻¹` the inner tendon reaches its limit and the outer tendon is at `−0.007`. Test either side and reverse the entity order. This checks active-limit detection without adopting any material default.

## Staffing, schedule and first handoff

**Planning scenario, approximately 10–12 weeks to a bonded preview.** It assumes one dedicated TypeScript/numerical developer, a bridge engineer supplying the adopted sources and calculations, an independent numerical reviewer and an independent qualified structural reviewer available at the relevant gates. The release approver owns the final disposition. Source procurement, collecting new experiments, reviewing a separate Korean design basis and service/member-level capabilities require separate scheduling.

| Weeks 5–7 | P4. Review nominal-to-Design traces for the rectangle, pure axial and transition cases. Missing normative decisions hold this milestone. |

**First handoff.** The bridge engineer supplies a rectangular cut and a box cut, tendon schedule including local slopes and loss stage, source properties for concrete, grout and both steels, design combinations with a sample FE action ledger, and the adopted code/errata plus owner exceptions. The developer freezes an RC baseline and demonstrates how the current readers handle an unknown field and an unknown version. The independent reviewers freeze the analytical expectations and the strength worksheet before comparing with production output.
